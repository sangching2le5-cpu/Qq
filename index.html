Unit 13 – Global Problems

Overview

An interactive self-study lesson for English majors (B1–B2). All original textbook content is preserved; Vietnamese explanations, IPA, definitions and extra examples are clearly marked as learning support.

Learning Objectives
  • Identify and pronounce vocabulary for disasters and tragedies.
  • Use verbs connected with disasters (erupt, spread, starve, shake, break out, sweep through).
  • Distinguish injure / damage and victim / casualty / survivor / refugee / migrant / wounded.
  • Talk about diseases and epidemics (rabies, AIDS, malaria, cholera, typhoid).
  • Build word families (verb → noun of thing → noun of person).
  • Complete exercises 13.1 – 13.5 of the coursebook.

A. Disasters / Tragedies

Vocabulary

Disasters not caused by human beings can be called natural disasters.

Language Help

Original

Injure /ˈɪndʒə/ is used about people.

Damage /ˈdæmɪdʒ/ is used about things.

200 people were injured. Many buildings were damaged.

Learning Notes (bổ sung)

• injure → tân ngữ là người: Two passengers were injured.

• damage → tân ngữ là vật: The flood damaged the bridge.

• wound /wuːnd/ cũng dùng cho người, nhưng thường do vũ khí trong chiến đấu.

C. Diseases / Epidemics

Vocabulary

Here are some headlines from newspapers all connected with diseases and epidemics.

• Rabies out of control in many parts of Asia — /ˈreɪbiːz/ disease can be caused by bite from a dog, fox, etc., very serious

• New AIDS unit to be opened this month — acquired immune deficiency syndrome; often caused by sexual contact or contact with contaminated blood

• New malaria drug tested — /məˈleəriə/ usually caught because of mosquito bites

• Cholera and typhoid injections not needed says Tourism Minister — /ˈkɒlərə/ /ˈtaɪfɔɪd/ diseases causing sickness, diarrhoea, etc., caused often by infected food and water

Learning Notes (bổ sung)

HIV là vi-rút gây suy giảm miễn dịch; AIDS là giai đoạn tiến triển của nhiễm HIV. Không phải mọi người nhiễm HIV đều mắc AIDS.
